# Information-theoretic readings of the shipped environment

Generated by `analyse_information_budget.py`. **No sampling, no seed blocks, no policy run.**
Every number is a closed-form function of (graph topology, prior rate table, edge
probability) under the simulator's *assumed* generative model. Bits below are properties
of that model, not measurements of a real review process.

Edge weight is the nominal `1 - exp(-lam)`; `weak_edges` / `strong_edges` scale the coin
rate by 0.6 / 1.4 to check that the ordering claims are not an artefact of one setting.

## The variable matters more than the formula

A review at node `j` reports `e_j` (is this node effectively wrong) — with
`P(flag | e_j=1) = p_catch` and `P(flag | e_j=0) = 0`, a Z-channel. But the repair clears
`b_j` (this node's *own* value). An inherited error survives the review. So

- `I(E_j ; O_j)` overstates what the agent can act on;
- `I(B_j ; O_j)` is the actionable version, with `P(flag | b_j=1) = p_catch` and
  `P(flag | b_j=0) = p_catch * inherited_j`.

Median `I(B)/I(E)` is **0.33 (real)** and **0.35 (ticket)**. About two thirds of the bits a
review delivers are about the variable the review cannot repair.

## The three candidate rankings disagree

`VoI_j = p_catch * b_j * delta_j`, where `delta_j` is the exact drop in expected wrong
conclusions when node `j`'s local value is cleared (finite difference on the forward OR
recursion; no approximation).

| rank by | real / nominal | ticket / nominal |
|---|---|---|
| argmax probability `p_j` | node 20 | node 17 |
| argmax entropy `H(E_j)` | node 10 (0.997 bits) | node 9 (0.997 bits) |
| argmax information `I(E_j;O_j)` | node 8 (0.763 bits) | node 9 (0.763 bits) |
| argmax repairable information `I(B_j;O_j)` | node 0 (0.735 bits) | node 0 (0.735 bits) |
| argmax `VoI` | **node 17** (0.0735) | **node 17** (0.0729) |

Spearman against `VoI`:

| statistic | real nom. | real weak | real strong | ticket nom. | ticket weak | ticket strong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `H(E)` | +0.156 | +0.652 | +0.120 | −0.116 | +0.260 | −0.155 |
| `I(E)` | +0.109 | +0.313 | +0.064 | −0.130 | +0.209 | −0.177 |
| `I(B)` | −0.044 | +0.191 | +0.001 | +0.562 | +0.543 | +0.619 |

The sharpest single fact: **node 10 on `real` has the highest entropy (0.997 bits) and the
highest review information (0.763 bits), and exactly zero value of information** — it has
no path to any conclusion (`sink_reach = 0`). Checking the most uncertain node is not a
worse version of checking the most valuable node; on this graph it is uncorrelated with it.

Node 17 is the reverse: only 11th most informative by `I(E)` (0.687 bits), first by `VoI`,
because it is the bottleneck in front of two conclusions.

## Budget ceiling

The actionable state `B` carries **14.31 bits** of entropy on `real` (12.09 on `ticket`).
Eight reviews, each at most 0.735 bits about its own node, give an upper bound of
**5.88 bits** — 41% (49%) of it. The bound is loose (sequential reviews correlate), but it
is a ceiling: the 8-review budget cannot resolve the repairable state even with perfect
allocation.

## Two things not to report

1. **`H(F)` for the final decision vector `F` is not identified by the environment.** The
   environment supplies per-node marginals (forward OR recursion is exact for marginals)
   and *one* joint — the mean-field one — while the reward only needs the marginals. Entropy
   is a functional of the joint. Any "residual entropy of the final verdict" number is a
   property of the chosen approximation, not of the simulator, let alone of the real queue.
2. **Review reliability is not a knob here.** `p_catch = 0.9` fixed, no false positives.
   The "high-reliability vs low-reliability reviewer" comparison has no instance in this
   family, so it cannot be illustrated with these runs.

## The shipped baseline is a derivative, not an action value

`marginal_scores` returns a reverse derivative of expected wrong conclusions w.r.t. the
source prior. Its per-node magnitudes differ from the exact one-step `VoI` by up to
0.21 in absolute terms (largest exact `VoI` anywhere in the six settings is 0.098), while its **ordering** survives:
Spearman `VoI ~ baseline` = 0.68–1.00 across the six settings. So: read `marginal` as a
ranking heuristic, never quote its score as an error reduction or as bits.

## Reading

Information theory is the right language for *why there is no headroom left*, not a new
objective for this environment. The prior planning ceiling (`dp - marginal <= 0.022` at
`K=5`) and the belief calibration result already say that allocating reviews better, or
estimating the belief better, buys almost nothing. What the accounting adds is the reason:
the budget is smaller than the repairable entropy, and most of the bits a review delivers
are about a variable the review cannot repair.
